The Whitefish rule

Evidence before enthusiasm. Control before capital. A field plan before a pitch.

Our screen

Every project has to survive the desk.

We organize scattered records into a decision: reject it, investigate it, or build a practical path toward execution.

Control

Leasehold, wellbore rights, working interest, net revenue interest, liens, assignments, and authority to operate.

Reservoir

Logs, mud logs, formation tops, completions, production history, nearby wells, and competing technical interpretations.

Well

Current mechanical condition, regulatory status, plugging exposure, equipment, access, power, water, and field constraints.

Route

Workover, recompletion, re-entry, restart, or phased field development—sequenced around what can actually be executed.

Economics

Real scope, contingencies, production cases, operating burdens, ownership allocations, and a clear use of funds.

Fit matters

We are selective on purpose.

A fast no is better than a slow maybe. We look for projects where diligence can change the outcome.

Worth a first look

  • Identifiable ownership and a party authorized to transact
  • Existing technical, production, or completion records
  • A well-defined operating problem or development question
  • Field access and infrastructure that can be verified

Usually not a fit

  • Unclear title, disputed control, or undocumented interests
  • Promotional projections unsupported by field evidence
  • No accountable operator or executable operating path
  • A request for money before diligence is possible
